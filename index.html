        A day in the life of a hostel block

        1. 03:00: water supply returns after an outage, and a tap that was left open starts to run.
        2. 03:08: the controller sees flow stay high for 8 minutes, closes the valve, and sends an SMS to the caretaker. About 623 L is saved compared with a 90-minute run.
        3. Morning: the caretaker reopens the valve and fixes the tap. The dashboard logs the incident and the saving.
        4. Later in the month: the quietest half-hour of the night stays above normal on two days in a row. The system flags a leak, a person confirms it, and that line is isolated until it is repaired.
        5. Month end: the block report shows litres used, litres saved, and the responsible-use score.
        PartBuilt and testedAt a real site
        Tap left runningIdle-flow rule, alarm after 8 minutesBuildable now. A pulse flow sensor with a 1 to 30 L/min range covers a running tap (the common YF-S201 type is listed at about ±10% accuracy). A normally open valve with a manual override cuts the supply.
        Leak detectionQuietest 30 minutes, tested with 0.05 L/min of sensor noiseUses a low-flow domestic or ultrasonic meter at the block inlet, because small leaks sit far below the 1 L/min that Hall-effect sensors can read. The threshold is then learned from 2 to 4 weeks of real night-time data.
        MessagesShown on this pageA NodeMCU or ESP32 controller sends readings by Wi-Fi or mobile data to a cloud database, and alerts go out by SMS or email. The rules run on the device, so cut-off still works if the network drops.
        Data-center coolingForecast and plan, tested on a simulated 1 MW siteRead load and weather from the building management system and the make-up water meter. Start in advice-only mode, then move to automatic control after a safety review. Needs hybrid or dry coolers on site.
        IncentivesScores for three blocks, pledge conceptPer-block meters feed the score. The pledge needs a partner, such as a school or a water charity, to hold the fund.

        Adapting it to other sites

        SiteWhat to measureWhat it controlsWhat changes
        Hostel or residential blockFlow at the block inlet and at the washroomsValve on the washroom lineLearn the daily pattern from term-time data, and keep a separate baseline for holidays.
        School or clinicInlet flow, plus tank level where tanks are usedValve on the supply to taps and tanksNights are quiet, so leaks stand out. Clinics need a manual override and no automatic cut-off to critical points.
        Office, hotel, or marketInlet flow and flow per floor or zoneZone valvesLarger flows need larger meters. Set the running-tap time from each zone own history.
        Data centerMake-up water, load, and outside temperature from the building systemCooling mode setpoints, advice firstNeeds hybrid or dry coolers. Savings follow the installed dry capacity (13% to 40% in the simulation).

        Pilot plan

        1. Bench test (2 to 3 weeks): sensor, board, and valve on a test rig. Calibrate the pulses-per-litre factor and check the tap rule.
        2. One-block pilot (4 weeks): meter and valve at a hostel washroom block. Alerts only for the first two weeks while the baseline is learned, then cut-off with a manual override.
        3. More blocks: add the score and pledge once the alerts are trusted.
        4. Data-center trial: advice-only on one cooling plant, with a partner operator.

        Pilot targets: fewer than one false alert per block per month, leaks confirmed within two days, and a measured water saving against the previous month. Safety rules: the valve fails open, a person confirms any line cut-off for a leak, cooling is never reduced below its evaporative floor, and only flow data is collected, never personal data.

Flow rate of 7.6 L/min for a 2.0 gpm faucet (EPA WaterSense). Cooling: a 1 MW hybrid site with 500 kW of dry coolers (assumed), 1.9 L per kWh for evaporative cooling (EESI average), and a heat wave and workload burst added on purpose. Blocks B and C are simulated peers. The 50 L per person per day benchmark is the basic requirement used in a Nsukka household study (Nigerian Journal of Technology).
